## part 1: what is a neural network?
[3blue1brown, neural networks chapter 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk&list=PLZHQObOWTQDNU6R1_67000Dx_ZCJB-3pi)

### terms
- **neuron**: holds a single number between 0 and 1, called its **activation**. for the input layer, that's a pixel's brightness, from 0 (black) to 1 (white).
- **layer**: a column of neurons. the network goes input layer (784 pixels, from a 28×28 image), then **hidden layers**, then the output layer (10 neurons, one per digit).
- **activations in one layer determine the activations in the next.** the brightest output neuron is the network's guess.
- **weights** $w$: one per connection between two neurons. they decide which pattern in the previous layer a neuron is looking for.
- **bias** $b$: one per neuron. it decides how high the weighted sum must be before the neuron becomes meaningfully active.
- **learning** = finding the right weights and biases.

### the intuition: layers break the problem into subproblems
pixels → edges → patterns (loops, lines) → digits

the second-to-last to last layer learns which combination of patterns corresponds to which digit.
(this is the *hope*. chapter 2 shows a trained network mostly doesn't do this, and checking what a network really does is exactly what mech interp is about.)

### one neuron
take every activation from the previous layer, compute a weighted sum, add the bias, then squish it into the range 0 to 1:

$$
a^{(1)}_0 = \sigma\left(w_{0,0}\,a^{(0)}_0 + w_{0,1}\,a^{(0)}_1 + \dots + w_{0,n}\,a^{(0)}_n + b_0\right)
$$

- $a^{(0)}_k$ is the activation of neuron $k$ in layer 0. the superscript is the layer, the subscript is the neuron.
- $w_{j,k}$ is the weight of the connection from neuron $k$ (previous layer) to neuron $j$ (next layer).
- the bias is *added*. a negative bias, like $b = -10$, means "only fire if the weighted sum is above 10".

### sigmoid (the "squishification" function)

$$
\sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}
$$

very negative inputs go to about 0, very positive inputs go to about 1, and $\sigma(0) = 0.5$.

### the whole layer at once (matrix form)
every neuron in layer 1 is connected to every neuron in layer 0, and each connection has its own weight. stack them up:

- activations of layer 0 → a column vector $\mathbf{a}^{(0)}$ with $n$ entries
- weights → a matrix $W$ with one **row per neuron in the next layer** (row $j$ holds all the weights going into neuron $j$)
- biases → a column vector $\mathbf{b}$

each row of $W$ times $\mathbf{a}^{(0)}$ is one weighted sum, so a single matrix-vector product computes all of them:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} w_{0,0} & w_{0,1} & \cdots & w_{0,n} \\ w_{1,0} & w_{1,1} & \cdots & w_{1,n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ w_{k,0} & w_{k,1} & \cdots & w_{k,n} \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} a^{(0)}_0 \\ a^{(0)}_1 \\ \vdots \\ a^{(0)}_n \end{bmatrix}
+
\begin{bmatrix} b_0 \\ b_1 \\ \vdots \\ b_k \end{bmatrix}
$$

then apply $\sigma$ to each entry. in short:

$$
\mathbf{a}^{(1)} = \sigma\left(W\mathbf{a}^{(0)} + \mathbf{b}\right)
$$

so the whole network is just this step repeated, layer after layer. it's a (very big) function from 784 numbers to 10 numbers.

### how many dials?
for 784 → 16 → 16 → 10:
weights $= 784 \cdot 16 + 16 \cdot 16 + 16 \cdot 10 = 12{,}960$,
biases $= 16 + 16 + 10 = 42$,
total $= 13{,}002$ parameters to learn.
